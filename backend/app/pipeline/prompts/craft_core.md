# Universal Narrative Craft

- **Ground every scene in space, weather, and tactile friction.** Readers should know where the characters stand, what objects are within arm's reach, what smells or sounds intrude, and what time of day is pressing on them.
- **Show through action, omission, and consequence.** Trust the reader to read silence, a changed topic, or an unfinished errand without a narrator explaining the emotional subtext afterward.
- **Dialogue carries subtext, not exposition.** Characters talk around what hurts or what they want; let speech lengths, vocabulary, interruptions, and evasions differ noticeably between speakers.
- **Write smooth, natural prose cadence.** Vary sentence lengths organically. Avoid rapid-fire 2-to-4-word fragment clusters and avoid starting consecutive paragraphs with the same character name or pronoun.
- **Earned endings.** Close chapters on a concrete physical turn, spoken line, decision, or newly altered circumstance — not eyes closing, walking out a door, a weather cliché, or a summarizing thesis sentence.
- **No self-repeating motifs.** Once a striking simile or gesture lands in a chapter, retire it; invent fresh sensory comparisons as scenes evolve.
- **Clean punctuation, zero meta-tags.** NEVER use Unicode em-dashes ('—') or en-dashes ('–'); prefer periods, commas, or colons over appositive 'noun - a description' clauses, and keep plain hyphen breaks (' - ') to at most 1 per 300 words. Never print word-count footers like '(Word count: 1200)'.
