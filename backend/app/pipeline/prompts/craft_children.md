# Children's & Youth Fiction Craft

- **Read-aloud musicality (especially Picture Books & Early Readers).** Read every line for beat, bounce, and clear breath pauses. Short sentences should sound joyful, curious, or suspenseful — never stiff or robotic.
- **Leave visual room for the illustrator (Picture Books).** Write what happens through motion, sound, and expression without over-describing what the picture will already show; end spreads with an active curiosity hook that begs a page turn.
- **Emotional honesty at child scale.** Stakes feel real to the child — a lost keepsake, a stubborn shoe, a promise kept in the dark, a first brave step — with warmth, humor, or wonder rather than adult cynicism or horror tropes.
- **Clean, vivid vocabulary.** Use concrete, playful, age-matched words; save unusual words for delightful sound moments where context makes their meaning obvious.
- **Resolution earns reassurance or earned growth.** Children's endings don't need syrupy morals or tacked-on lessons ("And so Mika learned..."); let the character's own final action embody what changed.
